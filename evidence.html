The problem, in numbers

Students are leaving school without a plan.

Theory of change

How we expect change to happen, one step at a time.

Each step builds on the one before. For every step we say what we do and what we measure, so we can tell whether it is working.

  1. Starting point

    Limited awareness

    Students know few careers, have few role models and feel unsure in formal settings.

    What we do
    Baseline recorded when students join
    What we measure
    How many careers and pathways each student can describe, and how sure they feel about deciding.
  2. Early change

    Self-knowledge and exposure

    Students can name their strengths and interests, and describe more careers and the routes into them.

    What we do
    DiscoverBuild
    What we measure
    Growth in careers described, and skills observed in practice: asking questions, explaining reasoning, contributing in a group.
  3. Decision point

    Informed choices

    The stream and course after Class 10 match students’ interests, not only marks or pressure.

    What we do
    DiscoverBridge
    What we measure
    The stream and course each student chooses, and whether it matches their stated interests.
  4. Longer term

    Pathways that hold

    Students stay on their chosen path, adapt to college or work, and regret their choices less.

    What we do
    Bridge
    What we measure
    Where students are a year later, whether they stayed on their path, and how they feel about their choice.

Discover, Build and Bridge are the three stages of our approach.

What we are still testing

The questions we don’t have answers to yet.

Studies show that career guidance raises students’ awareness and confidence. There is almost no rigorous data from rural India on whether it changes what students actually do. Our pilot is designed to help answer these:

?

Can better information outweigh family and financial pressure?

Students often decide under pressure from home and money. We want to see whether knowing more changes the choice they make.

?

Do skills built in a village school carry over to an urban college?

Confidence in a classroom you know is not the same as confidence in a new city. We will follow students to find out.

?

Do students regret their choices less?

We could not find a reliable estimate of career-choice regret among rural Indian students. The figures that circulate come from small, mostly urban studies or commercial surveys.

What we will contribute

We are running our programme as a careful pilot. We will track whether students who go through it make choices that better match their interests, and whether they stay on those paths. We will publish the results, including results that don’t go our way.
