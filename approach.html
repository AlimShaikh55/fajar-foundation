Our approach

We help students find out who they are, what’s possible and how to get there.

One programme, three stages. It starts in Grade 8, runs alongside school, and carries on after it.

Discover→Build→Bridge
How it works

Three stages, one student

01
Grades 8 to 10

Discover

Weekly sessions take students from self-discovery to career knowledge to planning.

  • Activities to identify interests and strengths
  • Exploring careers beyond the familiar handful
  • Conversations with local working professionals
  • Practice in weighing options before the stream choice after Class 10
02
Throughout

Build

Skills workshops, then practice inside every career activity, so students see why the skills matter.

  • Group discussions and presentations
  • Team projects with real roles
  • Hands-on computer, internet and AI use
  • Planning and goal-setting exercises
03
After school

Bridge

Support at the moments when students are most likely to drift.

  • Guidance on courses, colleges and admissions
  • Entrance exam preparation support
  • Mentoring through the first years of college or work
  • Tracking each student’s progress over time
What we build

The skills we build

Practised again and again, inside every activity, until they feel ordinary.

  • CommunicationSpeaking up in class, in interviews and with people outside the village.
  • TeamworkWorking with peers on real projects, sharing roles and settling disagreements.
  • CreativityComing up with more than one way to approach a problem.
  • Critical thinkingAsking why, weighing options and backing an opinion with reasons.
  • Problem-solvingBreaking a challenge into steps and trying again when the first attempt fails.
  • PlanningSetting a goal, mapping the route to it and tracking progress.
  • IT & AI skillsUsing a computer, the internet and AI tools safely and with purpose.
How we measure

We record a baseline, then follow the same students over time.

Three things tell us whether the programme is working:

Career awareness and confidence

How many careers and pathways a student can describe, and how sure they feel about deciding.

Skills, observed

Concrete behaviours, such as whether a student asks questions, explains their reasoning or contributes in a group.

What students actually do

The stream and course they choose, whether it matches their interests, and where they are a year later.
