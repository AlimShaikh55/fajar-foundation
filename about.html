Fajar means dawn. We started because too many students reach adulthood in the dark about their options.

Why we exist

In rural government schools, the decisions that shape a young person’s life — which stream to take, whether to continue after Class 10, what to study next — are often made by marks, family pressure or what friends are doing. Few students have met someone who does the work they dream of. Few schools have a career counsellor.

We believe a student from a village school should make these choices with as much information and confidence as a student anywhere else.

What we stand for

  • Students decide. We don’t tell students what to become. We help them find out, think it through and decide.
  • Start early, stay long. We begin in Grade 8 and stay in touch after students leave school.
  • Evidence over claims. We measure our work carefully and share the results honestly.
  • Rooted locally. We work in the districts we come from, in the language students speak.

Our mission

To prepare students in rural government schools to make informed career decisions and pursue them with confidence.

Our vision

A rural India where every student’s future is shaped by their own informed choice, not by chance.

Our team

Fajar is run by its founders, who give their evenings, weekends and alternate weeks to the schools we work with.

AS

Alim Shaikh

Co-Founder

Alim studied in a rural school, and that experience shapes everything he does.

Over seven years he has worked in education, youth development and public systems. He is a Gandhi Fellowship alumnus and has partnered with governments and communities to improve learning and to make programmes work on the ground. At Fajar he builds solutions that can grow, and that strengthen the local education systems students depend on.

KC

Kartik Chitrakar

Co-Founder

Kartik’s path into development began in Melghat, where Fajar works today.

For more than ten years he has worked with rural communities, most of all in Chhattisgarh, on livelihoods, gender and youth development. He also mentors rural students who are aiming for higher education. At Fajar he works to open clear pathways for young people to step into their futures.

NI

Nitin Ingle

Internal Governance Head

Nitin has spent four years working alongside tribal communities in central India.

His work spans climate resilience, rural governance and strengthening grassroots institutions. He has supported women farmers and community groups to build sustainable livelihoods and reach social protection. At Fajar he brings that field experience to community-led work.
