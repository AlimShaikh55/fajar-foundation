Rural students deserve to choose their future, not stumble into it.

Fajar Foundation works in rural government schools from Grade 8 onwards. We help students discover careers, build the skills to pursue them, and we stay with them after school.

Before dawn: how career choices get made today

SSC results are out at an ashram school in Melghat. A sixteen-year-old has a few weeks to choose Science, Commerce or Arts. She can name five jobs. No one she knows has done the one she half-wants. She picks what her friends pick.

A composite story, drawn from what we see in our schools.

  • 4 in 10

    rural young people aged 14 to 18 had no role model in the profession they hoped to join.ASER 2017 “Beyond Basics”, 28,323 rural youth

  • 19.9%

    of Scheduled Tribe students in Maharashtra drop out during Classes 9 and 10, against 11.5% of all students.UDISE+ 2024-25, Ministry of Education

  • 1 in 5

    students in Maharashtra do not move on from Class 10 to Class 11.UDISE+ 2024-25: transition rate 79.2%

  • 65.7%

    of India’s unemployed youth had secondary education or more in 2022, up from 35.2% in 2000. Finishing school is no longer enough.ILO & Institute for Human Development, India Employment Report 2024

These students are not short of talent. They are short of exposure, role models, guidance and confidence in unfamiliar settings. Read the evidence

What we do

We start early, before the big decisions, and we don’t leave when school ends.

  1. Grades 8 to 10

    Discover

    Students learn about themselves and the world of work: their interests and strengths, the careers that exist, and the routes into them. They meet working professionals and practise making decisions.

  2. Throughout

    Build

    Every career activity is designed to build transferable skills: speaking up, working in teams, thinking things through, planning ahead and using technology well.

  3. After Class 10 and 12

    Bridge

    We help students with admissions and entrance exams, mentor them as they settle into college or work, and track how they grow over the years.

Skills that travel with them

Rural students can communicate well at home and in the village. What they often lack is practice and confidence in formal settings: a college seminar, a group project, a job interview. We build that through repeated, supported practice.

  • CommunicationSpeaking up in class, in interviews and with people outside the village.
  • TeamworkWorking with peers on real projects, sharing roles and settling disagreements.
  • CreativityComing up with more than one way to approach a problem.
  • Critical thinkingAsking why, weighing options and backing an opinion with reasons.
  • Problem-solvingBreaking a challenge into steps and trying again when the first attempt fails.
  • PlanningSetting a goal, mapping the route to it and tracking progress.
  • IT & AI skillsUsing a computer, the internet and AI tools safely and with purpose.

Underneath all of these is agency: the confidence to make a decision about your own life and plan for it.

Where we are today

Small, honest, and just getting started.

We are a young organisation and we say so plainly. Today we work in two government tribal residential schools in Melghat, Amravati district. The work is unfunded so far.

Next, we want to bring the programme to more schools and reach more students across rural Maharashtra.

Our team

The people behind Fajar

Fajar is run by its founders, who give their evenings, weekends and alternate weeks to the schools we work with.

AS

Alim Shaikh

Co-Founder

Alim studied in a rural school, and that experience shapes everything he does.

Over seven years he has worked in education, youth development and public systems. He is a Gandhi Fellowship alumnus and has partnered with governments and communities to improve learning and to make programmes work on the ground. At Fajar he builds solutions that can grow, and that strengthen the local education systems students depend on.

KC

Kartik Chitrakar

Co-Founder

Kartik’s path into development began in Melghat, where Fajar works today.

For more than ten years he has worked with rural communities, most of all in Chhattisgarh, on livelihoods, gender and youth development. He also mentors rural students who are aiming for higher education. At Fajar he works to open clear pathways for young people to step into their futures.

NI

Nitin Ingle

Internal Governance Head

Nitin has spent four years working alongside tribal communities in central India.

His work spans climate resilience, rural governance and strengthening grassroots institutions. He has supported women farmers and community groups to build sustainable livelihoods and reach social protection. At Fajar he brings that field experience to community-led work.
